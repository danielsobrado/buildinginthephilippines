## Buying Construction Materials in China: Where Do You Actually Go?

Daniel here. OK so you've been browsing [Alibaba](/alibaba-sourcing), you've seen the prices, and now you're thinking... should I just go there myself?

Short answer: yes. But where do you actually go?

China is massive. Alibaba suppliers are spread across dozens of cities. You could spend months flying around and still miss stuff. So let me save you some time based on what I actually did.

## Why Foshan is the #1 City for Construction Materials

Seriously. If you're buying construction materials in China, Foshan is THE city. Tiles, sanitary ware, windows, doors, hardware, stone, furniture... it's all there. This is where most of the stuff you see in Wilcon and MC Home Depot actually comes from.

Foshan is just a few metro stops from Guangzhou, and Shenzhen is not far either. So you're in this whole Pearl River Delta area with a ridiculous concentration of manufacturers and showrooms.

Some people will tell you Shanghai has better companies, more premium stuff. And yeah I went to Shanghai too. There are interesting things there, more high-end brands. But honestly? Not THAT much more expensive than Foshan for what you get. And Foshan has so much variety that you could spend a week and still not see everything.

Priority should be Foshan. Always.

## Construction Showrooms, Wholesale Markets and Factories in Foshan

ok so there are basically 4 options and I tried all of them:

### 1. Red Star Macalline and Casa Construction Malls

These are like... imagine SM but only construction materials. Giant buildings, multiple floors, hundreds of showrooms.

Red Star Macalline (Hongxing Meikailong) is the biggest construction mall chain in China. Casa is another one. They're everywhere in Foshan.

Not bad for getting an overview. You walk around, see what's available, get a feel for pricing. The showrooms are nice, staff sometimes speak English (sometimes), and you can compare brands side by side.

But. These are retail-ish prices. Not terrible but not the best deals either. Think of it as window shopping with the option to buy.

Good starting point though. Spend half a day just looking around to calibrate your brain on what things cost.

### 2. Meiju Wholesale Ceramics Market - THIS IS WHERE YOU WANT TO BE

Meiju Ceramics Market (Meiju Jiancai Shi Chang). Remember that name.

This place is absolutely enormous. Like insanely big. Multiple buildings, thousands of suppliers, and everything is construction related. Tiles, marble, bathroom fixtures, kitchen stuff, you name it.

And the area around Meiju has even more markets. Zhongnan Market, Huaxia Ceramics City... it goes on and on.

Here's my advice: stick to the main ones first. Meiju and the surrounding markets. You will find that your time is used optimally there. Don't go running around to random suburbs looking for that one specific factory yet. The wholesale markets have showrooms FROM the factories, so you're basically visiting 50 factories in one building.

I spent days and days walking around these markets. Asking for prices, checking models, comparing quality. You can touch everything, see actual samples, negotiate on the spot. Way better than Alibaba photos.

### 3. Visiting Chinese Suppliers and Factories Directly

I did visit a lot of factories. Like a LOT. Tile factories, [window factories](/chinese-aluminum-suppliers), door factories, you name it.

My take? Only do this if you're really serious about one specific product. Like you've found something at Meiju that you love, you want to see the production line, maybe negotiate a bigger order directly.

Because honestly, most of the time the factory showroom is worse than their display at Meiju. And you lose half a day driving to some industrial zone in the middle of nowhere.

The exception is if you're ordering containers worth of stuff. Then yeah, go see the factory, check their quality control, make sure they're legit. But for your first trip? just stick to the markets.

btw a lot of the "suppliers" at Meiju ARE the factory. They just have a nicer showroom there. So you're kind of visiting the factory already, just the pretty version of it.

### 4. Canton Fair in Guangzhou

Everyone asks about this. The Canton Fair in Guangzhou happens twice a year (April and October) and it's the biggest trade fair in China.

Should you go? It could be a good idea. Lots of suppliers in one place, you can compare across categories, and some companies only show up at the fair.

BUT. It's way more expensive. Hotel prices near the fair go through the roof. Tickets cost money. The place is packed with buyers from all over the world. And honestly, for construction materials specifically, Foshan has more to see than the fair does.

If your trip happens to line up with the Canton Fair dates, sure, go for a day or two. But don't plan your whole trip around it. Foshan alone will keep you busy for days and days.

## Best Hotels Near Foshan Construction Markets

So I went with my kids (yeah, fun family trip to tile markets lol) and we stayed at the Venus Royal Hotel. Great hotel, really nice, and super well located for the markets.

Holiday Inn is another solid option, very well located too. And the prices in Foshan hotels are really good compared to Guangzhou or Shenzhen. We're talking like half the price for the same quality.

From either of those hotels you can walk to the main market areas or just take one metro stop. The metro system connects everything, Foshan to Guangzhou is seamless, same metro network basically.

## Tips for Buying Construction Materials in China

- WeChat Pay. Get it set up before you go. Cash is basically dead in China and some places don't even take cards
- Bring a portable phone charger. You will be on your feet all day scanning QR codes and taking photos of everything
- Take photos of EVERYTHING. Every price tag, every model number, every business card. You will forget what you saw by day 3
- Mandarin helps obviously but the market sellers are used to foreign buyers. Point, smile, use the calculator app to negotiate
- Get a local SIM or eSIM. You need VPN for Google and WhatsApp but WeChat works fine

### The Food Situation

Guangdong province has amazing food btw. Dim sum for breakfast every morning. Cheap and incredible. Don't eat at the hotel restaurant, find the local places. Your translator/fixer will know where.

Oh right, you should probably get a fixer. Someone local who speaks English and knows the markets. Ask around on expat forums or your Alibaba contacts might know someone. Makes everything 10x easier, especially for negotiating.

## Shipping Construction Materials from China to Philippines

So you found amazing tiles at half the price. Great. Now what?

You need a shipping agent. NOT the supplier's recommended agent (they take a cut). Find an independent freight forwarder in Foshan, there are tons of them. They handle consolidation, container loading, customs docs on the China side.

On the Philippines side you need a customs broker. This part is its own nightmare that I should write another article about...

If you haven't been to China yet and want to start sourcing online first, check out my [guide to buying on Alibaba](/alibaba-sourcing). And if you're specifically looking at [aluminum windows from Chinese suppliers](/chinese-aluminum-suppliers), I've written about that too.

Anyway. Foshan first. Markets first. Factories later. Canton Fair optional. Shanghai if you have time and want to see premium stuff but don't stress about it.

And bring comfortable shoes. Seriously. You will walk more than you've ever walked in your life.
